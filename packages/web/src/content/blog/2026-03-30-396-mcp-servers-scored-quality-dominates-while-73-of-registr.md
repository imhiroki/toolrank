The MCP ecosystem continues its upward trajectory with 396 servers now scored on [ToolRank](https://toolrank.dev), but this week's data reveals a critical gap that could be limiting AI agent adoption across the broader community.

## Ecosystem Health: Strong Quality, Growing Numbers

Our latest scan shows the MCP ecosystem maintaining impressive quality standards:

- **396 total scored servers** (up from previous scans)
- **85.7/100 average score** across all servers
- **254 servers (64%) achieve Dominant status** (85+ score)
- **133 servers (34%) reach Preferred tier** (70-84 score)
- **Only 9 servers (2%) fall into Selectable range** (50-69 score)

This distribution tells a compelling story: developers who properly implement MCP tool definitions are doing it well. The 98% success rate above the 70-point threshold suggests that when teams invest in proper tool documentation and implementation, they typically get it right.

## The 73% Problem: A Hidden Crisis in Tool Discoverability

The most striking finding this week isn't in our scored servers—it's in what we didn't score. Of over 4,000 repositories scanned from Smithery and the official MCP Registry, **approximately 73% contained no scoreable tool definitions**.

This represents a massive discoverability problem. Three out of four MCP projects are essentially invisible to AI agents that rely on well-structured tool definitions for integration decisions. While these projects may function perfectly when manually configured, they're missing the metadata layer that enables automatic discovery and evaluation.

For context, our [scoring framework](https://toolrank.dev/framework) requires basic elements like tool descriptions, parameter definitions, and usage examples. The 27% that do meet these criteria are performing exceptionally well, suggesting the barrier isn't technical complexity—it's awareness of best practices.

## Top Performers Set the Standard

This week's top 10 demonstrates what excellence looks like in MCP tool definitions:

**Microsoft Learn MCP** leads at 96/100, achieving perfect Function scores (25/25) and strong Clarity (34/36). The pattern holds across the top tier: **six servers achieve 95-96 point scores**, all maximizing their Function scores while maintaining high Clarity ratings.

Notably, servers like **Brave Search** (95/100) and **exa-mcp** (94/100) show that search and retrieval tools are particularly well-suited to MCP optimization, likely due to their clear input/output patterns and well-defined use cases.

## Quality vs. Quantity: The Implementation Gap

The stark contrast between our 85.7 average score and the 73% of projects with no scoreable definitions reveals two distinct developer populations:

1. **The 27% who implement tool definitions properly** and achieve consistently high scores
2. **The 73% who may have functional tools** but lack the discoverability layer that makes them valuable to AI agents

This suggests that MCP tool quality isn't primarily limited by technical capability—it's limited by adoption of documentation and metadata best practices.
